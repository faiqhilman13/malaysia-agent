## 1. PayNet rails: DuitNow Transfer, QR, Request, AutoDebit/Consent, DuitNow Online Banking/Wallets (FPX), cross-border QR. Who can reach the APIs?

### Takeaway
PayNet does run a public developer portal with a free sandbox. It documents DuitNow Request, DuitNow Consent/AutoDebit and DuitNow Pay (the online banking and wallet checkout replacing FPX). **Production access is limited to approved "Participants"**: banks, plus BNM-licensed non-bank merchant acquirers and e-money issuers that meet PayNet's thresholds. One example threshold is 1M+ transactions a year for non-bank acquirers. A startup that is not licensed has to reach these rails through an acquirer or gateway, and none of the gateways surveyed exposes DuitNow AutoDebit or Request generally yet (as of Oct 2026).

### Inferences
- **Architecture implication:** a non-licensed agent startup cannot become a PayNet participant. It can use PayNet's sandbox to learn message flows, but production DuitNow Transfer, Request, AutoDebit and Pay must run through a participant: a bank, a licensed acquirer or gateway (Curlec, GKash, Paydibs, Fiuu and others), or an EMI. The 1M-transactions/year bar for non-bank acquirers also puts direct participation out of reach for early-stage firms.
- **Request-to-pay and AutoDebit** are the rails best suited to "agent collects on behalf of business": the payer approves in their own app, and consent is a PayNet-native object. Gateway support was still "planned" in Aug-Sep 2026 and bank coverage of DuitNow Request is uneven. For near-term recurring collection, existing products are the practical route (Curlec direct debit/e-mandate, card auto-debit, e-wallet recurring).
- The FPX-to-DOBW migration is mostly a back-end change. Agents should treat "FPX" and "DuitNow Online Banking/Wallets" as the same checkout capability through gateways.

### Gaps
- Could not open PayNet docs pages to confirm whether a non-participant (e.g. a fintech with no licence) can register for sandbox certificates, or what the production onboarding checklist for DuitNow Request/AutoDebit acquirers involves.
- No authoritative list of which banks and e-wallets support DuitNow Request and AutoDebit as of Oct 2026.
- No official FPX sunset date, and no official PayNet launch date for "DuitNow Pay".
- China (Alipay+/UnionPay) and Vietnam/Philippines cross-border QR status unverified.

## 2. BNM open finance / open API: status, Financial Sector Blueprint 2022-2026 commitments, and third-party access to bank data

### Takeaway
BNM issued an **Open Finance Exposure Draft on 18 Nov 2025** (BNM/RH/ED 028-36). Comments closed 1 Mar 2026. **No final policy document was found as of Oct 2026**, and the Governor said on 8 Jul 2026 that rollout will be phased **from 2027**. PayNet is building the central infrastructure: an Open Finance Developer Portal is already deployed, and a pilot with seven banks plus EPF is slated for 2H2026. Phase 1 covers individual customers' account balances and 12 months of transactions (data sharing, i.e. read access), with SME data later. **No evidence of payment-initiation (PISP-style) rights was found.** Non-mandated firms may join only voluntarily, and the ED is silent on how they do.

### Cited Findings
- The ED was issued 18 Nov 2025 as a "foundational framework to facilitate consent-driven sharing of customer information across the financial sector". It says building the ecosystem "will be a multi-year journey" and links to the FSB 2022-2026 aim of a wider Open Data ecosystem — [BNM Open Finance ED (PDF)](https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/938039/ED_Open_Finance_2025.pdf)
- Mandatory participants: licensed banks, investment banks, Islamic banks, insurers, takaful operators, prescribed DFIs and eligible e-money issuers above customer thresholds. One summary reports these as >100,000 customers for banks and DFIs and >5,000,000 active users for EMIs. Others may participate voluntarily (Nov 2025-2026) — [Lexology summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e60ee322-92e4-4303-bf99-b4808e7a7366); [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/55022/various/bnm-open-finance-exposure-draft/)
- Rahmat Lim & Partners note that the ED "does not expressly mention when non-mandated financial institutions can participate" — [Rahmat Lim & Partners](https://www.rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/31748/bank-negara-issues-exposure-draft-on-open-finance)
- Phase 1 covers account and transaction information for individual customers, with SME data in phase 2. Largest banks' obligations are proposed from **1 Jan 2027**. Data-consumer duties start **1 Jan 2028** or when the FSP's para 8.4 obligations commence, whichever is later (secondary summaries of the ED) — [HHQ](https://hhq.com.my/posts/open-finance-in-malaysia-bank-negara-malaysias-exposure-draft/); [Lexology (HHQ)](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=7de1e3b5-ff6c-4645-8f58-a62251d3a5fa)
- Data scope reported: the last 12 months of transactions plus current account balance, shared only for customers who opt in — [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/malaysia-prepares-for-open-finance-rollout.html); [iMoney](https://www.imoney.my/articles/bnm-open-finance-malaysia-2026)
- Feedback closed 1 Mar 2026 — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e60ee322-92e4-4303-bf99-b4808e7a7366)
- The Governor said at AICB NEXUS on 8 Jul 2026 that Open Finance will roll out in phases from 2027, with infrastructure developed with PayNet and the industry — [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/54091/regtech-fintech-regulation-malaysia/malaysia-open-finance/); [MOF speech page](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/speech/aicb-nexus-2026-ai-trust-and-the-future-of-finance)
- PayNet has "already deployed its Open Finance Developer Portal". A live ecosystem pilot is scheduled for 2H2026 with seven commercial banks and EPF, and an RHB-sourced post expects first use cases by Q1 2027 — [The Edge: PayNet gears up for secure, consent-based data sharing](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/813725); [Fintech News Malaysia (EPF/Brankas)](https://fintechnews.my/60450/personal-finance-mgt-pfm/open-finance-malaysia-epf-savings-brankas-report/)
- Earlier (2025) reports targeted the pilot "as early as mid-2026", so the timeline has slipped — [iMoney](https://www.imoney.my/articles/bnm-open-finance-malaysia-2026); [Digital News Asia](https://www.digitalnewsasia.com/business/malaysia-advances-open-finance-strengthening-trust-inclusion-and-innovation-financial)
- Third-party projections of Q3/Q4 2026 finalisation and a ">1M customers first wave" come from a family-office guide, so treat them as unverified — [fiam.my](https://fiam.my/en/open-banking-malaysia-2027/)
- A BNM Assistant Governor called Open Finance "a key priority" under FSB 2022-2026 — [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/54091/regtech-fintech-regulation-malaysia/malaysia-open-finance/); FSB launch: [BNM press release (Jan 2022)](https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/fsb3_en_pr) [pre-2025]
- A successor plan exists: the Governor referred to a **2027-2030 Master Plan** with AI as a key pillar (Jul 2026) — [The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/business/124784/bnm-governor-financial-sector-to-enter-new-ai-driven-phase-under-2027-2030-master-plan)
- Industry aggregators already offer screen-scraped or credential-based bank connectivity, e.g. Finverse's CIMB Malaysia integration for individual accounts (undated) — [Finverse](https://www.finverse.com/banks/cimb-malaysia); Brankas is cited in open-finance coverage — [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/60450/personal-finance-mgt-pfm/open-finance-malaysia-epf-savings-brankas-report/)

### Inferences
- For a 2026-2027 agent startup, **regulated, consent-based bank-data access will not be generally available before 2027 at the earliest**, and for third-party data consumers probably 2028 or later. It starts with retail account and transaction data. SME/business account data, which is what reconciliation for businesses needs, comes in a later phase.
- Because no payment-initiation scope has been found, agents initiating payments from bank accounts will keep depending on DuitNow rails through participants, on bank corporate APIs/H2H, or on card-network agentic programmes, not on open-finance APIs.
- A startup wanting to be a "data consumer" should expect to need a regulated entity or partner. The ED's mandated parties are all licensed, and the route for voluntary non-mandated entities is unspecified.

### Gaps
- Could not read the ED PDF to confirm: data-consumer eligibility (whether non-licensed fintechs can register), the API standard and technical specification owner, liability, the consent dashboard, and whether payment initiation or write access is in scope.
- No final policy document or feedback statement located as of Oct 2026.
- No official BNM scorecard on FSB 2022-2026 open-finance deliverables.

## 3. Payment gateways and aggregators with developer APIs: DuitNow support, payouts, webhooks, MCP/agent toolkits

### Takeaway
Most Malaysian gateways expose REST APIs with webhooks for collection: FPX/DOBW, DuitNow QR, cards and e-wallets. Several also offer **payout APIs over DuitNow Transfer** (Billplz Payment Order, CHIP Send, Xendit Payouts). **Official MCP servers that work for Malaysian merchants are rare.** HitPay claims an official MCP server, and Stripe's MCP/Agent Toolkit exists globally but Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite excludes Malaysia. Razorpay's official MCP server is not shown to cover Curlec. Xendit, CHIP and Billplz have only community or third-party MCP tooling. Stripe in Malaysia covers FPX, GrabPay and cards, but **native DuitNow QR on Stripe is unconfirmed** and the sources conflict.

## 6. Licensing: FSA 2013 / Payment Systems Act 2003 / IFSA / MSBA; can an orchestration-only middleware operate unlicensed? e-KYC, RMiT, outsourcing; BNM AI discussion paper

### Takeaway
Under FSA 2013 (and IFSA 2013 for Islamic equivalents), **issuing e-money (a designated payment instrument) and operating certain payment systems need BNM *approval*** (s.11). **Merchant acquiring and some other payment-system operations need *registration*** (s.17). Remittance needs an **MSBA 2011 licence**. A middleware that never holds funds, never contracts with merchants to accept payment instruments, and never issues stored value is **plausibly outside these categories**. That is an inference: BNM tells applicants to self-assess against the merchant-acquiring definition, and there is no published safe harbour. Banks and gateways partnering with it will flow down RMiT, Outsourcing and (from Mar 2027) Technology Requirements obligations. BNM's Aug 2025 AI discussion paper is non-binding, and AI will be a pillar of the 2027-2030 Master Plan.

### Cited Findings
**Statutory categories**
- FSA 2013 distinguishes an "approved operator of a payment system" (approved under s.11 for systems in Schedule 1, Part 1) from a "registered operator of a payment system" (Schedule 1, Part 2, para 9). Merchant acquiring is a registered business. A "merchant acquiring service" is defined as an operator of a payment system "that enters into a contract with a merchant for the purpose of accepting payment instruments for payment of goods or services" — [FSA 2013 (BNM)](https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124///35ed2b4c-1995-f91d-3891-75d69d247d55); [FSA 2013 (InvestMalaysia PDF)](https://www.investmalaysia.gov.my/media/xrnl0vfp/financial-services-act-2013.pdf)
- BNM may gazette a "designated payment instrument" where it is widely used. Issuers of designated instruments need s.11 approval — [FSA 2013](https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124///35ed2b4c-1995-f91d-3891-75d69d247d55)
- BNM's application page says s.17 FSA requires registration for merchant acquiring, and advises applicants to assess whether their business model falls within the merchant-acquiring definition before applying — [BNM: Application for Approval and Registration](https://www.bnm.gov.my/application-for-approval-and-registration)
- A secondary source says a gateway that does not handle settlement or bear acquiring risk "may fall outside registration", but must still meet PCI DSS and its contract with the partner acquirer, and should seek BNM's view on borderline cases (marketing-oriented source) — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/merchant-acquiring-license-malaysia/)
- Merchant Acquiring Services PD: date and capital conflicts. One source says it was issued Sep 2021, effective Mar 2022 (banks) and Sep 2023 (non-banks); another cites an Apr 2023 version. Non-bank capital is RM300k per one source and "no fixed minimum" per another, so unverified — [Zitadelle](https://www.zitadelleag.com/news/malaysia-merchant-acquiring-license-bnm-guide); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/merchant-acquiring-license-malaysia/)
- Remittance and e-money are regulated under FSA 2013 and MSBA 2011. MSB licence class labels conflict across secondary sources (e.g. "Class B remittance only" vs "Category C") — [Azmi & Associates](https://www.azmilaw.com/insights/overview-of-the-regulatory-framework-for-money-services-in-malaysia/); [Remitso](https://www.remitso.com/blogs/how-to-start-money-transfer-business-malaysia)

**Technology, outsourcing and resilience rules**
- The **Technology Requirements PD for Payment Services Regulatees** was issued 12 Mar 2026 and is effective 12 Mar 2027. - An Aug 2026 analysis says BNM viewed the existing technology-neutral framework as broadly adequate, with room for stronger supervision if risks grow (secondary) — [Utopia Group](https://utopiagroup.com.my/ai-news/bnm-ai-malaysian-financial-sector-discussion-paper)
- The Governor at AICB NEXUS (8 Jul 2026) said "AI is a tool… ultimately dependent on human judgement and oversight" and that banks must keep humans accountable for AI decisions. He backed an industry-led AI governance framework from AICB's CRO Forum ("the banking profession governing itself") and cited AI as a pillar of the 2027-2030 Master Plan — [MOF speech page](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/speech/aicb-nexus-2026-ai-trust-and-the-future-of-finance); [Fintech News Malaysia](https://fintechnews.my/59532/ai/bnm-ai-banks/); [The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/business/124784/bnm-governor-financial-sector-to-enter-new-ai-driven-phase-under-2027-2030-master-plan)

### Inferences
- **Orchestration-only middleware (no funds held, no merchant acquiring contract, no stored value, no cross-border remittance):** probably no BNM licence needed. The risk points are:
  1. Contracting with merchants to accept payments, i.e. acting as a payfac or sub-merchant aggregator, which may be read as merchant acquiring under s.17.
  2. Holding or pooling client funds, even briefly, which risks e-money or deposit-taking characterisation.
  3. Moving money cross-border, which falls under MSBA remittance.
  4. Under open finance, acting as a "data consumer", which will need whatever participation route the final PD sets.
  A formal BNM consultation or legal opinion is advisable before launch. **This is inference, not a verified legal position.**
- In practice, the startup will be a **third-party service provider to licensed entities**. Banks will apply RMiT and Outsourcing PD due diligence. Non-bank acquirers and EMIs will apply the Technology Requirements PD from 12 Mar 2027, and their tiering by volume affects how heavy those flow-down obligations are. Expect audit rights, data-residency and cloud questions, exit plans and incident reporting in partner contracts.
- BNM's emphasis on human accountability points to a human-in-the-loop requirement for consequential actions. In partner banks' eyes that could include agent-initiated payments above thresholds. Approval workflows, mandate limits and audit logs should be designed in from the start.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the s.11 and s.17 texts, Schedule 1 lists, or the current Merchant Acquiring Services PD (version and capital).
- No BNM guidance located on "payment facilitators", technical service providers, or AI agents acting under customer credentials.
- BNM e-KYC PD (latest version), PDPA 2010 amendments (2024-2025) and cross-border data transfer rules were not researched due to the search budget.
- Payment Systems Act 2003: background knowledge says it was repealed and replaced by FSA/IFSA 2013, but no source was retrieved this session, so verify.

## 7. Market data: DuitNow/QR volumes, e-wallet usage, cash vs digital (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Malaysia is digital-heavy and accelerating:
- BNM counted **18.4bn e-payment transactions in 2025** (+25% y/y), or **538 per capita**.
- PayNet processed **8.44bn transactions** in 2025, with non-bank volumes up **71.7%**.
- **DuitNow QR doubled to 3bn transactions** in 2025, across 3M+ acceptance points.
- Cross-border QR reached 29.7M transactions (2.5x).
Precise cash-share data for 2025 is scarce.
